# Forest Post Café — Game Concept

**Status:** Early Development / Concept Stage  
**Platform:** Windows PC  
**Genre:** Cozy Simulation / Exploration / Story-driven Adventure  
**Engine:** Not selected  
**Document intent:** Creative direction, not a feature-complete specification.

## 1. Premise
A quiet forest post office has been left to the player, along with a tiny adjoining café. The building has seen better days; village connections have faded. The player becomes both a caretaker and a messenger, delivering stories as much as parcels. Small daily rituals and thoughtful visits slowly bring warmth back to the community.

## 2. Emotional promise
**A little care can make a place feel like home.** The experience should feel restorative, personal, and full of gentle discoveries. Players should feel welcomed rather than tested, and leave each session with a sense of having made someone’s day a little better.

### Design pillars
1. **Unhurried freedom.** No combat, countdown-based pressure, or harsh failure states. Exploration and small detours are part of the experience.
2. **Human-scale stories.** Letters naturally connect the player to NPCs, histories, and evolving relationships. Emotional storytelling should emerge through modest, believable acts.
3. **Comforting rituals.** Café preparation, post sorting, bicycle journeys, and evening reflection create a satisfying daily rhythm.
4. **Visible belonging.** The forest and café gradually become warmer, more cared-for places through meaningful progress.
5. **Cohesive, readable world.** Consistent Classic Low Poly scenery invites curiosity without visual noise.

## 3. Proposed core loop
1. **Morning — open.** Enter the café, welcome neighbors, and prepare drinks.
2. **Sort — choose.** Read delivery requests, organize mail and parcels, and plot a manageable route.
3. **Prepare — pack.** Put letters and packages in the bicycle basket.
4. **Travel — explore.** Ride trails between woodland locations. Discover shortcuts, side stories, and scenery.
5. **Deliver — connect.** Give items to recipients and unfold small character scenes.
6. **Restore — progress.** Unlock community improvements, new connections, and optional postal routes.
7. **Evening — return.** Enjoy the café, customize the space, review keepsakes, and close the day.

No exact number of daily deliveries, customer interactions, or destinations is committed at this stage.

## 4. Planned systems — proposals, not implementation
### Postal deliveries
Simple, legible letter/package sorting; flexible route selection; story-linked recipients; occasional optional side errands. **Prototype question:** how much route planning adds meaning without becoming an efficiency chore.

### Bicycle travel
An approachable ride with an emphasis on observation and peaceful traversal. **Prototype question:** handling and camera should be comfortable with a keyboard/controller; cycling must feel delightful at low speed.

### Café routines
Prepare a compact menu of drinks and care for a cozy room. Avoid punishing orders and frantic management. **Prototype question:** how interactive should preparation be without becoming repetitive.

### Narrative and villagers
An ensemble of recurring NPCs; stories unfold through messages, conversations, and personal gestures. The writing should favor warmth, curiosity, and quiet complexity over melodrama.

### Environmental progression
Gardens, trail signs, weathered structures, and gathering spots can improve as connections are renewed. Progress should be legible without grind.

### Decoration and collectibles
Small café customization and postcards from meaningful places. Cosmetic choices are expressive rather than stat-optimized.

## 5. World proposals
| Area | Identity | Potential storytelling function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forest Café | Home base, mail sorting, hospitality | Everyday rituals and familiar encounters |
| Pine Trail | Dappled shade, pines, wayfinding signs | Introductory trips and slow discovery |
| Lakeside Village | Waterside cottages, docks, bridge | Neighbor relationships and interwoven stories |
| Flower Hill | Open meadows and gentle views | Reflection, postcards, quiet optional moments |
| Old Postal Route | Overgrown former delivery road | Memory, reconnection, gradual restoration |

Every area is conceptual; boundaries, scale, and routes will be decided in prototyping.

## 6. Tone and pacing
Warm, wistful, and gently hopeful. Calm does not mean empty: varied sights, conversations, weather, and small surprises can make a short journey memorable. No enemy encounters, stressful timers, or harsh penalties are part of the intended design.

## 7. Interaction and accessibility goals
- Clear and readable interactable silhouettes.
- Comfortable, low-pressure controls and camera motion.
- Legible subtitles and flexible dialogue pacing.
- Color choices that do not rely on hue alone to communicate essential information.
- Audio atmosphere that complements rather than overwhelms the scene.

These are goals to be tested, not confirmed settings.

## 8. Target first playable slice
A future vertical slice could include: one café interior, one short connected forest route, one bicycle, a few letters, two or three recurring villagers, one simple café interaction, and one visible community improvement. Exact scope will be defined after basic interaction prototypes.

## 9. Out of scope for initial repository setup
Game engine selection, gameplay scripts, packages, executable builds, save systems, dialogue content, and commercial promises. This repository initialization provides presentation and preproduction references only.

## 10. Open design decisions
- Best riding and exploration camera perspective.
- Whether café drink-making is hands-on or primarily ritualistic.
- Route planning depth and player autonomy.
- How daily transitions affect story progression without pressure.
- How postcards and decorative rewards connect to the narrative.

This document is a starting point; update it after playtesting and design decisions.
